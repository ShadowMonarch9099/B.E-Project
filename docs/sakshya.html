Sakshya साक्ष्य

Sakshya means “evidence”. It answers questions over an office’s own English, Hindi and Marathi files, including old scans. It replies in the language and script the person typed in, and every sentence points to the page it came from. When the files don’t support an answer, it says so and shows the closest pages it found.

01 · Review of your proposal

Your synopsis points the right way. The plan and the deck drift away from it.

I read the abstract, both synopsis versions, both research-gap reviews, the detailed plan v2 and the Review 1 deck. The six paper PDFs in the folder are empty (0 bytes), so the paper-level analysis below relies on your gap review’s summaries of them.

Keep

What is already strong

  • RQ1–RQ4 are well framed and measurable
  • Page-level metadata on every chunk
  • PostgreSQL + pgvector + HNSW is right-sized
  • Cross-encoder reranking after dense recall
  • The synopsis metric list (citation P/R, abstention precision, OCR robustness)
At risk

What a reviewer will attack

  • Three documents describe three different systems
  • Novelty rests on a feature multilingual-E5 already has
  • “Eliminates hallucination” is claimed, never measured
  • SaaS work absorbs half the team’s 12 weeks
Change

What Sakshya changes

  • The “adaptive router may…” line becomes the core contribution
  • Handles how people in Maharashtra actually type: romanized and mixed
  • Abstention becomes a calibrated gate plus per-sentence checks
  • A small human-checked benchmark replaces synthetic-only evaluation
Critical

The plan, deck and synopsis disagree on what you are building

Plan v2 is a multi-tenant SaaS that calls Claude Haiku with dense-only top-5 retrieval over 12 weeks. The Review 1 deck promises QLoRA LLaMA-3.2-3B, 100+ languages and 6 months. The synopsis is a controlled comparison of three retrieval strategies with abstention. An examiner who reads two of them will ask which one is real.

Fix: adopt the synopsis framing as the single source of truth and rewrite the plan and deck to match it.

Critical

The claimed novelty is a property of the model you download

Plan v2 names “the multilingual embedding layer (multilingual-E5) that allows cross-lingual retrieval” as the novel contribution. Cross-lingual retrieval is what multilingual-E5 was trained to do. The one idea that would be yours, an adaptive router, appears only as “may select”.

Fix: make the learned router (N1) and the romanized-query path (N2) the headline contributions.

Critical

Hallucination control is a prompt instruction

Plan v2 says the system prompt “explicitly forbids answering outside the provided text, eliminating fabrication”. Small models ignore such instructions regularly, especially in Hindi and Marathi. The plan has no mechanism to catch it and no metric that would show it.

Fix: an evidence gate before generation, an NLI check on every sentence after it, and an unsupported-claim rate in the results table (N4).

High

Citations can be wrong by construction

RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter with 512/64 windows runs over extracted text and will produce chunks that span two pages, so the “page” on a citation is sometimes the wrong page. Plan v2’s ingestion also lists only pdfplumber, python-docx and TXT; OCR for scans appears in the synopsis but not in the build steps.

Fix: chunk inside page boundaries, keep bounding boxes, and add Tesseract with per-word confidence to the ingestion pipeline.

High

langdetect will fail on your real users’ input

Short queries, Hindi vs Marathi (both Devanagari), and romanized text like “kiti divas miltat” are exactly where langdetect is unreliable. Every downstream choice (routing, answer language) depends on this label.

Fix: AI4Bharat IndicLID for native and romanized script, IndicXlit for transliteration, and a Hindi/Marathi check on the corpus side.

High

Three heavy training jobs, one of them from scratch

Fine-tuning E5-large, training a cross-encoder from bert-base-multilingual-cased on 3–8k pairs, and QLoRA on a 3B generator all on a T4 is a lot for one member. A reranker trained from a base BERT on a few thousand pairs usually underperforms an existing multilingual reranker. LLaMA-3.2’s official language list includes Hindi but not Marathi.

Fix: fine-tune a pretrained multilingual reranker, train the router (cheap and novel), and pick the generator by a small Marathi/Hindi pilot. QLoRA becomes a stretch goal.

High

Evaluation data can leak

Plan v2 generates 2–5k queries with GPT/Gemini for fine-tuning and evaluates on “a held-out set” built the same way. IndicCorp and Wikipedia dumps are raw text with no question–evidence labels, and nothing tests unanswerable questions.

Fix: a ~400-question human-checked set with page-level gold evidence and a 15% unanswerable slice.

Medium

Unsupported numbers in the deck and plan

Slide 2 cites “73% of enterprise data” and “3.5 hrs avg. daily time spent searching” with no source. Plan v2 predicts “~12% P@5 improvement” before any experiment. Examiners notice both.

Fix: cite a source or remove the statistics, and turn predicted gains into hypotheses.

Medium

Indic lexical search needs its own tokenization

The plan’s hybrid route relies on PostgreSQL full-text search, which ships no Marathi configuration. Scanned government orders also often print numbers in Devanagari digits (१४२), which will never match “142” typed in a query.

Fix: IndicNLP normalization, character n-grams for OCR noise, and digit normalization in both directions.

02 · The proposed solution

One pipeline, five places where the design is new

The ingest side runs once per document. The ask side runs per question and snakes from the router to the answer. Boxes outlined in violet carry a novelty tag, explained in section 05.

Direct multilingual route Translate-to-English route Hybrid dense + BM25 route Novelty component

Models you train (your ML core)

  • Router: gradient-boosted trees on ~15 query and corpus features, trained on oracle labels from the RQ1 experiment
  • Reranker: fine-tune a pretrained multilingual cross-encoder (e.g. mMiniLM trained on mMARCO) with your hard negatives
  • Evidence gate: logistic calibration over reranker and retrieval signals
  • Verifier (optional): fine-tune mDeBERTa XNLI on Hindi/Marathi claim–evidence pairs

Models you use as they are

  • multilingual-E5 (base vs large as a budget variable)
  • IndicLID, IndicXlit, IndicTrans2 (AI4Bharat)
  • Tesseract with hin and mar traineddata
  • A 2–4B instruct model in 4-bit via llama.cpp or Ollama, picked by pilot
  • Optional ablation: BGE-M3, which gives dense and sparse scores from one model

Delivery, kept small

  • FastAPI + PostgreSQL/pgvector, as you planned
  • Next.js single workspace: upload, ask, PDF viewer with highlighted evidence
  • “Why this route” panel showing router features and scores
  • Multi-tenancy, JWT, S3, Redis, billing move to future work

05 · Novelty

What is new, and which gap each piece closes

Each item maps to a gap your review found in the seven papers, and to a hypothesis you can test in section 07.

N1 · Learned retrieval router

The comparison study becomes a policy the system uses

Run all three routes on every training question, record which one found the gold page best, and train a small model to predict that from cheap features: script, romanization, code-mix ratio, numbers and IDs in the query, query length, corpus language mix, share of scanned pages. At query time it picks a route, or sets fusion weights, in under a millisecond.

Closes the gap in Translation-based embeddings (2024) and Resource-efficient Indic RAG: neither says which queries need translation. Tested by H1 and H2.

N2 · Romanized and code-mixed queries

Accepts the way people really type

Many users type Marathi or Hindi in Latin letters and mix in English terms (“ATKT”, “backlog”). Sakshya builds three views of each query (as typed, transliterated to Devanagari, translated to English) and normalizes Devanagari digits. The router sees all three.

None of the seven reviewed papers evaluates romanized or code-mixed queries. Tested by H3.

N3 · OCR-aware retrieval and citation

Knows when its evidence came from a bad scan

Tesseract word confidences are averaged per chunk and stored. They feed the router, shift fusion weight toward character n-grams on noisy pages, and put a “check the page” badge on citations from faint scans.

Closes the gap in Multilingual IDP system (2025) and M3-RAG: no page-cited QA evaluated against scan quality. Tested by H4.

N4 · Claim-level verification with calibrated abstention

Every sentence is checked against the page it cites

A gate decides before generation whether the evidence is enough, which skips the LLM entirely when it isn’t. After generation, a multilingual NLI model checks each sentence against its cited chunk. Unsupported sentences are dropped, and if too few survive the answer becomes an abstention. The threshold is set from a risk–coverage curve.

Closes the out-of-context failure reported in LegalRAG (2025). Tested by H5.

N5 · Script-faithful answering

Answers in the language and the script of the question

Romanized Marathi in, romanized Marathi out, with a Devanagari toggle. The output is checked for language and script. On mismatch it is transliterated or translated with IndicTrans2 rather than returned in English.

Addresses the query-language and Latin-script bias in Language Preference of Multilingual RAG (ACL Findings 2025), and gives RQ4 a mechanism. Tested by H6.

C6 · Sakshya-Bench

A small public benchmark for Indian office documents

About 400 human-checked questions over ~60 English, Hindi and Marathi documents (native and scanned), with page-level gold evidence, a romanized slice and an unanswerable slice. Releasing it is a contribution on its own, and it removes the synthetic-data leakage risk.

No reviewed paper releases an Indic private-document QA set with unanswerable questions.

The question every examiner will ask

“Hybrid search, NLI checking and transliteration already exist. What is new?” The answer: the router is trained on the outcome of your own controlled comparison, so the study produces a working policy instead of a table. And the system is evaluated under conditions the reviewed papers skip together: romanized queries, scanned Marathi, page-level citations, unanswerable questions and a T4 budget.

09 · Risks, and what to fix in your documents now

Where this can go wrong, and the fallback for each

Annotation takes longer than planned

Start in week 1. Cap at 400 questions. Let an LLM draft questions, but have a person confirm every evidence page.

The router overfits 400 questions

Keep it shallow with about 15 features, cross-validate by document, and report the gap to the oracle openly. A modest gain over the best fixed route is still a result.

Small models write poor Marathi

Generate in Hindi or English and translate with IndicTrans2, then report fidelity and fluency separately.

The NLI verifier is weaker in Marathi

XNLI covers Hindi but not Marathi. Fine-tune on 1–2k translated and hand-checked claim pairs, and report per-language verifier accuracy.

Tesseract struggles on old scans

Treat it as a finding: the recall-vs-OCR-quality curve is part of H4. Keep the OCR engine fixed so the comparison stays fair.

T4 memory runs out

Use E5-base vs E5-large as a budget variable, ONNX int8 for embeddings, and a 4-bit GGUF generator. Load models once per process.
